## Overall readout

The employee-needs framing and broad content groupings were received positively in stakeholder reviews. Feedback predominantly added useful improvements rather than challenging the structure. The immediate challenge is to make the proposed CX front door understandable to employees unfamiliar with CX terminology, clarify what each page offers, and ensure its links lead to useful authoritative destinations. **Converge on a usable first release; do not reopen the information architecture by default.**

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **Plain language and discoverability.** Labels such as “See how Customer Experience…”, “CX Intel”, “customer measures”, “customer modes/segments” and “Build your Customer Experience skills” need employee-centred explanations or plainer language. Some stakeholders explicitly sought “learning”, “complaint handling” and campaign-related language they would search for. [~01:13; ~10:49; ~18:38; ~63:00; ~66:00]
2. **Visible structure and scanning.** The “Understand who our customers are” page initially looked like one long block rather than distinct offerings. Consider stronger section labels, space/padding, and a short up-front overview; slim the research section to a few current, useful resources. Keep a meaningful “why it matters” and related pathways. [~12:50–19:00]
3. **Show available resources, not perpetual ‘coming soon’.** Remove or defer the draft CX strategy offer until there is a real destination; “coming soon” language was viewed sceptically. Surface a small number of actual research learnings instead of an empty or overlong library. [~16:25–19:10]
4. **Make the employee action and destination explicit.** Start descriptions with what employees can do or find, not internal definitions. Explain link destinations, any access prerequisites, and distinguish external resources from internal navigation. The CX intelligence dashboard has an access/request and data-confidence learning path that should be accurately described. [~20:00–24:00]
5. **Tell a connected story across the site.** Explain the relationship between measures/results and customer feedback (“CX Intel” vs “CX Voices”), including where a colleague supporting customers can find customer feedback. Cross-link genuinely related pathways rather than duplicate material. [~21:00–24:00; ~68:45–69:20]
6. **Avoid promoting unclear request pathways.** Participants raised concern about the usability/reputation of Service Hub. A deep link may reduce navigation friction but does not fix form experience. The group reaffirmed the rationale for consistent intake to prioritise requests. Validate exact destination, contact language and current policy; do not create parallel ad hoc channels. [~56:00–60:00]
7. **Validate offer boundaries with owners.** Suggestions included outbound/SMS and seasonal campaign support, translation-related assistance, complaint-handling learning, and requests for ad hoc Salesforce reports. These are signals to clarify the service offer and route, not commitments to expand scope. Dennis cautioned against ungoverned bespoke reporting when core measures and feedback channels are intended to be preferred. [~59:00–71:00]
8. **Trust requires content governance.** Stakeholders asked who maintains content, when it is reviewed, and which resources are authoritative. Quarterly review was discussed as an initial reference, but fast-changing capabilities may need more frequent checks. Explicitly establish accountable owner, publishers, cadence, links and escalation. [~23:00–24:00; ~71:00; ~117:45–118:50]

## Implication for delivery

**Before publishing:** make every page answer three questions quickly: *What can I do here? Where will this take me? Who keeps this information current?* The structure is promising; confidence will come from understandable wording, reliable pathways and active stewardship.
