## Executive snapshot for closure

The project translates the CX branch offer into a clearer employee-need navigation experience: **employee need → concise CoMWeb guidance → authoritative source or specialist service → useful next step**. User-reported go-live occurred on 18 November. Seven supplied final HTML page exports were inspected; they provide evidence of configured pages and links, not end-user functional PVT. A 26-resource CX document upload was reported; library metadata, permission and CoMpanion retrieval checks across all 26 remain to be confirmed. CoMpanion routing guidance and a Microsoft Forms employee-feedback survey have been prepared, but live activation and PVT results are not proven.

The initial T0 seven-day SharePoint Analytics capture (12–18 November) records 310 combined page views over the seven CX pages and 45 summed **page-level** unique viewers (not 45 distinct people). It includes pre-launch traffic and editing/testing and must not be presented as post-launch uptake or successful customer journeys.

The next phase is **stabilise, learn, hand over and close**, not broaden the redesign.

## Retrospective — 75-minute facilitation design

**Participants (confirm):** CX project delivery contributors, publishing representative, content owners, CoMpanion/knowledge administrator and relevant user-research representatives.  
**Intent:** Learn from delivery and identify repeatable ways of working; avoid a personal performance review.

- 0–10 min — Timeline: discovery → prototype → page approval → 18 Nov cutover; evidence and surprises.
- 10–25 min — What helped employees and the team (findability, source linking, content decisions, publishing).
- 25–40 min — What slowed delivery (late governance, folder/view constraints, naming reversals, link and AI source dependencies).
- 40–55 min — What outcomes can we actually prove? Contrast shipped outputs versus employee-use evidence and CoMpanion PVT.
- 55–70 min — Prioritise improvements using effort and employee benefit; assign no more than 3–5 actions.
- 70–75 min — Confirm action owners, capture decisions and route remaining work to BAU or a separately approved enhancement.

**Prompts:** Which decisions were made too late? Which controls reduced rework? Did “link, don’t duplicate” simplify future maintenance? How should AI navigation be governed alongside human navigation? Which verification task should become a standard launch gate?

**Output:** A one-page lessons and actions record, with evidence separated from hypotheses.

## Executive summary — proposed structure

1. **Why we did it:** fragmented employee access to CX help/resources; employee-need navigation rather than organisational structure.
2. **What changed:** seven-page L1/L2 experience, revised document access/grouping, source-oriented guidance, CoMpanion routing and a survey/analytics measurement loop.
3. **What shipped:** describe verified production artefacts, their URLs, and approvals; do not overclaim the 26/26 PVT or AI activation.
4. **What evidence says:** discovery/interviews, post-publish PVT, survey results and comparative analytics; distinguish usage from actual employee task success.
5. **What is unresolved:** source/legacy exclusions, BAU ownership, accessibility and link defects, naming convention deferral.
6. **What leadership needs to decide:** acceptance, ongoing owner and resourcing, prioritised next improvements, sunset dates for obsolete content.

Aim for a two-page executive readout plus links to the evidence/artefact register.
