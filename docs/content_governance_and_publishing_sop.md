# CX Content Governance and Publishing SOP

Status: Draft / working process  
Owner: Project Manager  
Last updated: 7 October 2026

## Purpose

Define a practical, repeatable process for moving CX employee content from an identified need or change through review, approval, publication, maintenance and retirement.

This SOP supports the CX SharePoint Refresh but is intended to remain useful after the project team transitions out.

It does not replace organisational CoMWeb, SharePoint or Digital governance. Where organisational governance applies, it remains authoritative. This document translates agreed requirements into an operational CX publishing process.

## Operating principle

The refreshed experience should not depend on project knowledge or a single person remembering how content is managed.

The process should make it clear:

- why content exists;
- which source is authoritative;
- who owns its accuracy;
- where it belongs;
- who reviews and approves it;
- who publishes it;
- how publication is checked;
- when it must be reviewed again; and
- when it should be updated, linked, archived or retired.

## End-to-end process

```text
Need or change identified
        ↓
Confirm employee need and purpose
        ↓
Confirm authoritative source
        ↓
Identify accountable content owner
        ↓
Prepare or update content
        ↓
Review accuracy and suitability
        ↓
Validate placement / information architecture
        ↓
Obtain required publishing approval
        ↓
Publish
        ↓
Post-publish QA
        ↓
Record owner + review trigger/date
        ↓
Maintain, update, link, archive or retire
```

## 1. Identify the need

A publishing request should begin with a clear employee need, required correction or validated organisational change.

Before creating new content, check whether:

- authoritative content already exists elsewhere;
- the employee need can be met by linking to an existing source;
- the change is correcting, updating, consolidating or retiring existing material; or
- a new CX content object is genuinely required.

Default to simplification and linking rather than duplication.

## 2. Confirm the authoritative source

Before drafting or publishing, identify where the underlying information is governed.

Record:

- authoritative source or system;
- responsible business area or content owner;
- whether CX owns the information or only provides a pathway to it; and
- dependencies on other content, systems or inbound links.

If source authority is unclear, do not treat the content as publication-ready.

## 3. Confirm ownership

Every published CX content object should have an accountable owner for accuracy and currency.

The owner is responsible for confirming that the information remains correct. The owner does not necessarily need to be the person who writes or publishes the content.

Where ownership cannot be established, escalate the decision before publication or treat the content as a candidate for linking, archiving or retirement.

## 4. Prepare the content

Content should be prepared in line with:

- the agreed employee need;
- current CX experience and content design;
- one-authoritative-source principles;
- accessibility requirements;
- agreed tone and language;
- SharePoint / CoMWeb constraints; and
- any applicable organisational content standards.

Content should not be copied from an authoritative source merely to make it easier to publish locally when a maintained link would meet the employee need.

## 5. Review and validate

Before approval, validate two different questions:

**Accuracy**
- Is the information correct and current?
- Has the accountable content owner reviewed it?
- Does it accurately represent the authoritative source?

**Experience and placement**
- Does it belong in this location?
- Does it meet the employee need?
- Does it duplicate existing content?
- Are navigation, links and inbound pathways understood?
- Is it accessible and usable?

These checks may involve different people.

## 6. Publishing approval gate

Content is ready to publish only when the required controls have been satisfied.

```text
Content ready?
      ↓
Authoritative source confirmed?
      ↓
Content owner approves accuracy?
      ↓
Placement / IA validated?
      ↓
Required publishing authority approves?
      ↓
Publish
      ↓
Post-publish QA
      ↓
Owner + review trigger/date recorded
```

The exact organisational approval roles are still subject to current CoMWeb / Digital governance and project confirmation. Do not infer approval authority from access to the publishing platform.

## 7. Publish

The authorised publisher implements the approved change in the relevant employee-facing environment.

Publication should preserve, where applicable:

- agreed page structure and navigation;
- links to authoritative sources;
- important existing inbound pathways;
- accessibility requirements; and
- traceability to the approved content or change.

## 8. Post-publish QA

After publication, verify at minimum:

- the page or resource is accessible to the intended audience;
- links work;
- content appears in the intended location;
- navigation and important inbound pathways still work;
- the published content matches the approved version; and
- obvious accessibility or formatting issues have not been introduced.

Material defects should be corrected before the change is considered complete.

## 9. Record maintenance responsibility

For published content, record enough information to support ongoing maintenance:

| Control | Minimum requirement |
|---|---|
| Content owner | Accountable person, role or business area |
| Authoritative source | Source or system the content depends on |
| Publisher / publishing function | Who can implement approved changes |
| Approval requirement | Who or what approval is required before publishing |
| Review trigger or date | When currency should be checked |
| Status | Active, update required, linked, archived or retired |

Avoid creating maintenance fields that nobody will use.

## 10. Review, update or retire

Content should be reviewed when:

- the authoritative source changes;
- the responsible business area identifies a change;
- a scheduled review is due;
- employee evidence indicates the content no longer meets the need;
- a link, system or service changes;
- duplication is identified; or
- ownership can no longer be established.

Use the smallest appropriate action:

**Keep → Update → Link → Archive → Retire**

Do not preserve content only because it already exists.

## Governance and decision rights

The project should validate the following minimum decision rights before transition to business-as-usual.

| Decision | Required accountability |
|---|---|
| Why the content exists | Employee need / business owner |
| Whether information is accurate | Accountable content owner |
| Which source is authoritative | Relevant business owner / organisational governance |
| Where content belongs | CX experience / information architecture with applicable Digital governance |
| Whether content can be published | Applicable publishing authority |
| Who implements the change | Authorised publisher |
| Whether content remains current | Accountable content owner |
| Whether content is archived or retired | Content owner plus applicable governance / publishing authority |

Names should only be added once the role is confirmed. The operating model should survive personnel changes.

## Project Manager contribution

Within the CX SharePoint Refresh, the Project Manager's focused contribution is to:

1. document the actual publishing process as this project uses it;
2. capture governance and decision rights without inventing policy;
3. make publishing approval gates explicit;
4. test the SOP against real Deliver 1.0 publishing activity;
5. refine the process from observed exceptions and handoffs; and
6. hand over a usable operating model for governance and maintenance.

This contribution does not make the Project Manager the permanent content owner, publisher or approval bottleneck.

## Validation through Deliver 1.0

Do not wait until the final governance phase to design the process theoretically.

Use the first release as the test:

| Phase | SOP activity |
|---|---|
| Define and Develop | Draft the process and identify unresolved governance decisions |
| Define and Deliver 1.0 | Run real content changes through the process and capture gaps |
| Transition to governance and maintenance | Confirm roles, approvals, review triggers and handover |

The SOP becomes reliable through observed use, not documentation alone.

## Open governance questions

The following remain to be confirmed through the project and relevant organisational governance:

- final publishing authority for each content home;
- final approval pathway for new versus amended content;
- accountable owner model for shared or cross-team content;
- review cadence or event-based review triggers;
- where ownership and review metadata will be recorded;
- retirement and archive authority;
- how CoMWeb / Digital governance requirements apply to each content type; and
- any additional controls required for high-risk or organisation-wide content.

## Relationship to project risks

This SOP is intended to reduce:

- **R-001** — unclear content ownership;
- **R-005** — refreshed content becoming outdated again; and
- **R-006** — ambiguity about authoritative sources.

It also supports **R-007** by making post-publish pathway and link checks part of the process.

This is a project working artefact. It captures the intended operational process for validation during the refresh.
