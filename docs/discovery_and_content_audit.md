# Discovery and Content Audit

Status: Draft  
Current phase: Define and Develop (23 September–13 October 2026)  
Last updated: 30 September 2026

## Purpose

Capture current-state evidence, employee research, content findings, benchmarking, Digital input and unresolved questions.

This page records discovery. Future-state design decisions belong in `experience_and_content_design.md`.

## Discovery streams

### Current-state review

Review existing purple, green and black pages to understand:

- content;
- duplication;
- gaps;
- ownership;
- currency;
- current structure.

### Benchmarking

Review relevant internal spaces to understand:

- navigation patterns;
- content structure;
- access models;
- how employee needs are or are not reflected.

Benchmarking is an input, not a requirement. Patterns should be tested against employee need, governance and platform feasibility before adoption.

### Employee research

Twelve employees across CX were interviewed to understand:

- how they currently find CX information;
- whether and how they use SharePoint;
- what makes information feel reliable;
- what they expect CX SharePoint to provide;
- how tools, resources and support fit into day-to-day work.

### Digital alignment

Engage Digital to understand:

- where organisation-facing CX content could live;
- how CoMWeb, Digital Dashboards and other spaces fit together;
- governance and maintenance expectations;
- how CoMpanion affects findability.

## Current-state findings

### 1. Much of the content is outdated

Pages do not consistently reflect current teams, structures or work.

### 2. Overlap creates uncertainty

Similar information exists across multiple pages, making it unclear which source to use.

### 3. Useful content is harder to find

Valuable resources are often text-heavy, fragmented or buried.

## Benchmarking findings

### Team-first by design

Many internal SharePoint spaces centre on team information, internal work and rituals.

### Repository-led structure

Navigation often mirrors organisational structure rather than employee need.

### Restricted by default

Many spaces are permissioned, reinforcing a team-first rather than organisation-wide model.

## Employee research findings

### 1. Needs-led, not browse-led

Employees usually arrive with a task or need rather than browsing SharePoint.

### 2. The CX offer is unclear

The current experience explains who CX is but not clearly what employees can come to CX for.

### 3. Trust matters as much as findability

Employees need to know whether content is current, authoritative and the right version.

### 4. Useful resources and clear next steps

Employees value tools, guidance, services and support that help them get something done.

### 5. Different needs require different experiences

Organisation-wide resources, branch knowledge, working files, specialist content and support do not all need to live in the same place.

## Emerging opportunities

1. Clarify how CX can help.
2. Make tools and resources easier to access.
3. Make trusted information easier to identify.

## Current content-home hypothesis

### CoMWeb / purple

Organisation-facing content for a broad employee audience.

### Digital Dashboard / green

Specialist, role-specific, branch or team content, working material and permissioned resources.

### Linking principle

Where content is authoritative elsewhere, provide context and link to it rather than duplicate it.

## Content status definitions

**Keep** — current, useful and suitable with little or no change.  
**Update** — useful but requires change.  
**Remove** — no longer provides sufficient value.  
**Archive** — retain for reference but remove from active experience.  
**Link** — authoritative content lives elsewhere; provide context and link.  
**Review** — insufficient evidence to decide.

## Evidence classification

**Reported** — raised by a stakeholder but not independently validated.  
**Observed** — directly seen in the current experience or content.  
**Validated** — confirmed through sufficient evidence or responsible owner.  
**Open** — still requires investigation.  
**Future signal** — potential future need that should not yet be treated as a requirement.

## Open questions

- What is the primary role of CX SharePoint within the future CoMWeb model?
- Which content should be organisation-facing versus specialist / internal?
- Which source is authoritative where content overlaps?
- What content should not be migrated?
- Which current entry points and CoMpanion links need to be preserved?
- What content ownership gaps remain?
- Which employee tasks need explicit CX guidance or next steps?

## Research limitations and outstanding work

The 12 interviewees were employees across CX. Findings should inform design, but do not establish organisation-wide prevalence or validation by employees outside CX. No quantitative task-success or findability baseline is reported in the deck.

The complete content inventory, additional purple pages, inbound-link audit, final ownership, final information architecture and implementation feasibility remain unresolved.
