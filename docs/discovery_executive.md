# Discovery and Content Audit

Status: Discovery outcomes consolidated; legacy inventory reconciliation outstanding  
Last updated: 27 November 2026

## Executive summary

Discovery identified a fragmented CX information environment, with outdated content, overlapping sources and resources that were difficult to find. Research and Digital alignment informed a shift from team-based navigation to an employee-needs-led experience.

| Evidence | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 12 CX employee interviews | Employees seek useful resources, trusted information and clear next steps rather than browsing organisational structures. |
| Approximately 55–60 pages reported in October audit discussions | Significant rationalisation opportunity; differing snapshots, not a verified retirement count. |
| Internal benchmarking and Digital input | Simplify navigation, retain authoritative sources and support multiple discovery pathways. |
| 31 reconstructed content-register entries | Evidence-backed dispositions and dependencies; complete legacy inventory and owner validation remain outstanding. |

## Findings and design implications

| Finding | Response |
|---|---|
| Outdated or duplicated content | Refresh priority guidance and link to authoritative sources instead of copying. |
| Unclear CX offer | Explain what CX helps employees achieve. |
| Fragmented resources | Provide contextual routes and quick links. |
| Inconsistent trust and ownership | Define maintenance responsibilities and publishing controls. |
| Different employee needs | Support CoMWeb, specialist sources, search and CoMpanion. |

## Implementation and learning

Seven refreshed page exports were inspected following the reported 18 November go-live; 26 resources were reported uploaded. These are delivery indicators, not proof of employee task success or complete legacy retirement. Six post-launch feedback submissions generated seven proposed actions. Validate priority feedback, employee access, link integrity and ongoing ownership during closure.
